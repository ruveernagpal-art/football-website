football facts

The 31-0 International Match: The highest-scoring official international game took place in 2001, when Australia defeated American Samoa 31-0. Australian forward Archie Thompson also set an individual record by scoring 13 goals in that single match.

Messi's Unstoppable 2012: Lionel Messi holds the record for the most official goals scored in a single calendar year, netting a staggering 91 goals in 2012 for Barcelona and Argentina.

The Ultimate Goalscoring Goalkeeper: Brazilian keeper Rogério Ceni spent his entire career at São Paulo FC and scored 131 goals, completely shattering records for any player in his position by primarily converting free-kicks and penalties.

Unprecedented Club Dominance: During ronaldos peak years with Real Madrid, he managed a mind-boggling ratio, scoring 451 goals in just 438 competitive games—averaging well over a goal per game across nine seasons.

The Billion-Follower Icon: Off the pitch, cristiano ronaldo is arguably the most recognizable person on Earth, becoming the first athlete to cross 1 billion combined followers across social media platforms like Instagram, YouTube, and X.

The Teenage World Cup Record: At the 1958 World Cup, a 17-year-old Pelé became the youngest player to score a hat-trick (against France) and the youngest to play and score in a World Cup Final (against Sweden). Both records remain unbroken.

The Three-Time Champion: pele remains the only player in football history to win three FIFA World Cups as a player (1958, 1962, and 1970).

The Unfairly Targeted Genius: Because defender-protection rules were loose in his era, Maradona holds the record for the most fouls sustained in a single World Cup game (23 fouls against Italy in 1982) and the most overall in a single tournament (53 fouls in 1986).

the Ultimate Five-Minute Sequence: In the 1986 quarter-final against England, maradona scored the infamous "Hand of God" handling goal, and just four minutes later, dribbled past five players over a 60-meter stretch to score the FIFA-voted "Goal of the Century".

The Hat-Trick of Ballons d'Or: johan Cruyff was the first player in history to win the Ballon d'Or three times (1971, 1973, 1974), establishing his utter dominance over European football in the early 1970s.

The Signature Deception: He is one of the very few players to have a fundamental football skill named after them. The "Cruyff Turn"—a fake cross followed by pulling the ball behind the standing leg—was introduced to the global stage at the 1974 World Cup and is taught to children worldwide today.
